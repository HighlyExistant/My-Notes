The way we define things is paramount to how we discuss them. There are three parts to defining something
1. ==**Term**==: Identify the word of phrase being defined
2. ==**Class**==: Identify the class of object or concept to which the term belongs
3. ==**Differentiating Characteristics**==: Differentiate characteristics that distinguish it from all others of its class.$^{(1)}$
## Examples
* Water (_term_) is a liquid (_class_) made up of molecules of hydrogen and oxygen in the ratio of 2 to 1 (_differentiating characteristics_).$^{(1)}$
## Types of Definitions
A definition can either be a **positive definition**, or a **negative definition**.
* ==**Positive Definition**==: Defines an object through characteristics, classes and terms. Defining an object on what the object is conceptually.
* ==**Negative Definition**==: Defines an object by what it is not.
Typically when making definitions, it is preferred to have a strong positive definition, but there are some cases where a negative definition might be more suitable.
