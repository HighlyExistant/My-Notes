## Background
* She was alive during 5$^\textrm{th}$ century Athens.
* She was the mistress of the statesman Pericles who said quote: "It is better for woman not to be mentioned".
* She was Pericles' political adviser
* Philosopher
* She came from Miletus as the name suggests
* Fathers name is Axiochus
* She married the Athenian politician Lysicles
* She was born Circa 470 B.C.
* She came to Athens Circa 450 B.C.
* She was a metic.
* Metic's:
	* Could not own land in Attica.
	* Could not participate politically in Attica.
	* Punishment for falsely presenting as a citizen was enslavement.
* The date and place of her death is unknown.
* Many of the criticisms that arise of her, is of having borne the bastard Pericles Junior.
* While many authors of old comedy would blame her for the start of the Peloponnesian war, the author states: "Aspasia's alleged responsibility for the outbreak of the Peloponnesian War sounds suspiciously like the charge attributed to Duris that she was responsible for the Samian War. I believe that the accusation is fictional and groundless."
## Regarding the Status of Metic Women
Metic woman, since they could not enter a fully valid Athenian marriage, were subject to the following legal unions:
* ==**Kyrieia**==: Greek word for Lord, here however it means **the legal ==guardianship== of a woman by her nearest male relative or husband**.
* ==**Kyrios**==: Here it refers to **a ==guardian== who will represent the metic and will arrange a marriage, most likely to another metic, or to place her in the third option**.
* ==**Pallakia**==: concubinage with citizen of Athens.
# The Story Told by Comedy
* Attic Comedy is a genre full of commentary on politics, sexuality and power.
* The way Aspasia is portrayed in these texts is very sexualized, which although other males such as Pericles also had their sexual integrity attacked, they also had their oratorical skills, physical appearance and political views attacked.
* It is said that Aspasia is the only female to have the derogatory word **Katapygosyne** used to describe them. 
## In regards to Cratinus
* Founder of old Political Comedy, "Cratinus can be considered the founder of political comedy as we know it" (20), ==although other sources say it was Aristophenes==.
* Other sources say his plays were cruel and outright abusive.
* His plays contained characters which served as allegories with their characters.
	* Zeus: Pericles
	* Hera: Aspasia
## In regards to Eupolis
* He mentions Aspasia by name in 3 comedies
* In the play ==_Prospaltians_ she is mentioned as Helen==. Most likely because both were "accused of sexual impropriety and were alleged to have started wars".
* In ==_Philoi_ she is called Omphale tyrannon== who is said to have sexually dominate Heracles, "whose earlier cultural image as hard drinking beast master and womanizer was now being sanitized and assimilated to an icon of temperate masculine virtue".
* One of his final plays labels Aspasia as just a whore, referring to her inferior sexual status.
## In regards to Hermippus
* "according to Plutarch, it was he who prosecuted Aspasia for ==asebeia==".
* "He also is said to have accused Aspasia of ensnaring free women for Pericles to dally with, a charge also leveled, without a named accuser, against Phidias"
* It is said that the plays of Hermippus were also abusive and mysoginistic as were all the other old comedies.

# Vocabulary

* ==**Fellatrix**==: a woman who performs fellatio
* ==**Procuress**==: A woman who obtains other woman as prostitutes for another person.
