* ==machtpolitik==: **A doctrine in political theory advocating the use of power and especially of physical force by a political state in the attainment of its objectives**. Its etymology is derived from German, =="macht" being power in German==. The example the book uses is of **Thrasymachus (Trasímaco)** who appears in the dialog "La Republica". Its opposer **Callicles** supports ==realpolitik== meaning **a system of politics or principles based on practical rather than moral or ideological considerations** bases itself on compromise and using diplomacy.
* ==Thomism==: Called that way due to the works of **Thomas Aquinas**, Came from the need to prove the existence of god through logical proofs. The most recent version of Thomism is known as ==Neo-Thomism== and it came as a countermovement to contemporary philosophers like Immanuel Kant.

* ==esoteric==: to be understood by an obscure few (obscure knowledge).

* ==heliocentric==: The theory that the planets spin around the sun and not the other way around.
* ==geocentric==: The theory that the earth is the center of the solar system. It has already been disproven.
* ==arete==: **moral virtue**.
* ==agathon==: **good or virtuous**.
* ==episteme==: **science and knowledge, also known as the branch of philosophy concerning knowledge**. "Aristotle divides all science into the theoretical which seeks knowledge for its own sake".
* ==poietic==: **making, producing, forming**: Aristotle divides this as "the general principles of practical life and art". **However before we achieve the end, this end must be known**.
* ==techne==: The care or cultivation of something, rationally guided for the sake of achieving some legitimate end.
### Plato's view of Prometheus
Plato views art as an intrinsic need for humans to exist. For this reason Prometheus needed to bring art with him at the beginning of human history. Prometheus is often remembered for bringing fire, but he also brought with him art, writing, mathematics, agriculture, medicine and knowledge (science). 

The etymology of Prometheus is pro- Fore, and -metheus is thought, therefore Prometheus is forethought, and by bringing all of these gifts to humanity, he is giving them forethought on what they can create. 

His brother who causes a bunch of problems is called Epimetheus, Epi- meaning after, meaning he is afterthought. He only learns after the fact.
### Plato's view of "Technique"
There is no modern word that captures the definition of **techne**. Modern usage of the words art, are meant to capture the creation of beautiful things, while craft captures the practical or things that are useful, but to Plato "the useful is beautiful or fine, and the hurtful ugly or base.", and as such **techne** really covers both of these definitions. The reason we cannot view technique as a proper translation either is due to it being referred to the individual proficiency in the whole endeavor. This is followed up by ==Plato's view of art==: They refer to painting and sculpture as **higher arts** as the person doing them must look at their work and analyze what must be done at every stage, while woodworking, stonecutting etc, as a lower art, as it corresponds to following measurements and instructions. **Higher art is qualitative, while lower art is quantitative**. He differentiates the artist from the technician, again giving them a negative connotation. He states that. To truly master the art you must:
1. Must know more than just the rules of procedure (the instructions to create something), but instead understand how this structures acts in basis to other things, and how it responds to different treatments. **"If you simply know the conditions to produce an effect, then no matter how sensational you are, at best you're a copyist"**, instead you should understand the whole if you are to master the art.
2. Possess the rules of procedure governing action. What this means is that if you are to craft something, **that thing you are creating has a way of being made. The way it is made may vary in shape or size, but there is a procedure in which that thing is made**. If you are making a shovel, you can't just put it the way you desire, as it has a general form which you view in your mind. **All *techne* is rationally guided and therefore if the shovel you are making breaks, you should be able to throw it away and remake your shovel, not basing yourself out of an already existing shovel, but out of the form you have in your mind "If you have any skill at all".**.
3. The artist must know not only this theory of procedure, but also be able to practice it.

Technique also seems to carry negative connotation, and Plato views it as the degeneration of art or craft
# Properties of *Techne*
1. The **useful end** on account of which the art exists
2. The **work** or the concrete art that can serve this purpose
3. the general **form or structure** which every such work must exemplify if it is to meet the end
4. the technical **procedure** by which this form is imposed.
5. the **concrete matter** in which the form is imposed
If the properties 1, 2 and 3 are gone, and only 4 and 5 remain, it turns into a **technique**.
## Warning against *False Techne*
When the **useful end** gets accidently substituted or confused, then it can lead to a false techne, as it will corrupt the whole. If the end is bad, the whole is bad.
### Example
* A health manufacturers useful end is to provide their consumers with drugs that help cure their ailments. If instead their drugs begin to not cure, but cause pleasure in their patients, they may portray themselves as healthy, but it is only a facade.
* Another example is **sophistry** which produces the facade of knowledge.
