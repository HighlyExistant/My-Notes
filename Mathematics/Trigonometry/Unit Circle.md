The unit circle is a circle who's radius is equal to $1$, therefore its equation is $$x^2+y^2=1$$
To get coordinates on this circle using an angle, you can use the following trigonometric functions:
* $cos(\theta)$: The $x$ coordinate in the unit circle.
* $sin(\theta)$: The $y$ coordinate in the unit circle.
* $tan(\theta)$: Gives the tangent, that is the slope of the $x$ and  coordinates and is equal to $\frac{sin(\theta)}{cos(\theta)}$. Due to division by $0$, $\theta \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$.
* $sec(\theta)$: The reciprocal of $cos(\theta)$.
* $csc(\theta)$: The reciprocal of $sin(\theta)$.
* $cot(\theta)$: Gives the cotangent, that is the inverse slope of the $x$ and $y$ coordinates and is equal to $\frac{cos(\theta)}{sin(\theta)}$. Due to division by $0$, $\theta \neq n\pi$.
## Trigonometric Identities
* $sin^2(\theta)+cos^2(\theta)=1$.
# Properties of Sinusoidal Waves
let $\Psi$ be a a sinusoidal wave $cos$ or $sin$. With all its transformations it equals
$$
	A\Psi(B(\theta+C))+D
$$
* ==**Amplitude**==: Here $|A|$ represents the amplitude of the function. Basically the maximum and minimum points the function will take. The [[Functions|range]] would be $[-|A|+D, |A|+D]$
* ==**Period**==: Is when the function starts to repeat itself, which is every $2\pi$. We can change this value with $B$ where it will then start repeating itself every $2\pi B^{-1}$.
* ==**Phase Angle**==: Is the displacement of the phase, which would just be $-C$.
* ==**Average Value**==: Would be the total average of a wave, which is usually $0$ unless it is summed on the outside. therefore the average value is $D$.
* ==**Interval of the first cycle**==: Would be $[-C, -C + 2\pi B^{-1}]$ 
# Ley de Senos
for a triangle with $a, b, c$ and their opposite angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ we can see that: $$\frac{sin(\alpha)}{a}=\frac{sin(\beta)}{b}=\frac{sin(\gamma)}{c}$$
# Area de un Triangulo
Si tienes la longitud de dos lados $b, c$ y tienes un angulo $\alpha$ que esta formado por esos dos lados, puedes sacar el area como: $$
\frac{1}{2}bc sin(\alpha)
$$
## Formula de Heron
con lados $a, b, c$
$$
	A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}
$$
$$
	s=\frac{1}{2}(a+b+c)
$$
