The end goal of cellular reproduction is to convert 1 cell into more cells. This is done through two processes **Mitosis** and **Meiosis**, where such reproduction corresponds to body cells and gametes (sexual cells) respectively. The process of both is very similar and can be represented by the acronym ==**PMAT**== where it goes from:
1. Prophase: *Pro* meaning before.
2. Metaphase
3. Anaphase
4. Telophase
# Vocabulary
* ==**Diploid**==: Means that it contains 2 sets of chromosomes. Represented as $2n$.
* ==**Haploid**==: Means that it contains 1 set of chromosomes. Represented as $n$.
* ==**Homologous Pairs**==: Approximately the same size and contains the same type of genes in the same locations. ^6cda56
* ==**Chromatid**==: One of the two threadlike strands from the chromosome, which contains DNA.
* ==**Chromosome**==: a threadlike structure of nucleic acids and protein found in the nucleus of most living cells, carrying genetic information in the form of genes.
# <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">Mitosis</mark>
Mitosis only goes through ***PMAT*** once, so the end result is 2 cells.
## Prophase
* the chromosomes become visible and are said to be ==**condensing**== meaning that they are thickening.
## Metaphase
* The chromosomes align each other to the center of the cell
## Anaphase
* Here the chromatids are being pulled away to opposite sides of the cell via the ==**microtubules**==.
## Telophase
* Now that the chromatids are in opposite sides of the cell, a new nuclei is forming to prepare for the creation of a new cell.
## Cytokinesis
* Pulls the cell to finally separate into two cells.
## Finished Product
After mitosis you will be left with ==**two equivalent cells**==.
# <mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">Meiosis</mark>
Meiosis only goes through ***PMAT*** twice, so the end result is 4 cells.
## Prophase I
* the chromosomes become visible and are said to be ==**condensing**==.
* The chromosomes get paired with their [[#^6cda56|homologous pairs]] where each chromosome corresponds to a different parent. Here they start ==**crossing over**== genes from one pair to another.
## Metaphase I
* The chromosomes align each other to the center of the cell in the same pairs as prophase I.
## Anaphase I
* Here the chromosomes are being pulled away to opposite sides of the cell.
## Telophase I
* Now that the chromosomes are in opposite sides of the cell, a new nuclei is forming to prepare for the creation of a new cell.
## Cytokinesis I
* Pulls the cell to finally separate into two cells.
## Prophase II
* the chromosomes become visible and are said to be ==**condensing**==.
## Metaphase II
* The chromosomes align each other to the center of the cell
Anaphase
* Here the chromatids are being pulled away to opposite sides of the cell.
## Telophase II
* Now that the chromatids are in opposite sides of the cell, a new nuclei is forming to prepare for the creation of a new cell.
## Cytokinesis II
* Pulls the cell to finally separate into two cells, forming four cells in total
## Finished Product
After mitosis you will be left with ==**four variant cells**==, that are haploid.

# When is Mitosis Dangerous
Neoplasms are anormal tissues, caused by a loss of control on the cellular reproduction process, reaching very high densities of these neoplasms. These could be caused by malicious tumors. There's two types of tumors.
* ==**Benign Tumors**== or Benign Neoplasm or Benign Growth: Is a noncancerous collection of cells. These don't spread to other parts of the body, and grow slowly.
* ==**Malignant Tumors**==: Are clumps of cancerous cells that do spread to other parts of the body. These tumors usually have a high amount of [[Molecules#^1a597f|telomeres]] which lead to rapid cell division.
