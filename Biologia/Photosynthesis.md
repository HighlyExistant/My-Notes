Etymology: *Photo* meaning Light, *synthesis* into glucose.
# Steps:
## Capturing Light
* ==**Using pigments**== (ej. chlorophyll) you can capture the light energy to be used. These are found in the ==**chloroplast**== where photosynthesis occurs.
## <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">Light Dependent Reactions</mark>
These happen in the ==**thylakoids**==. A collective stack of these thylakoids is called a ==**granum**==, while multiple of these stacks is called a ==**grana**==. 
* Here the light captured is used and the water that was captured by the plant is split $$\text{Light }+ 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{ATP}+\text{NADPH}$$
## <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Light Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)</mark>
Using the resources of the light dependent reactions, these reactions will instead occur outside of the thylakoids and in a fluid called the ==**stroma**==. Here carbon dioxide is taken in through pores in the ==**stomata**==. 
* <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">ATP</mark>: Works as an energy currency for the Calvin Cycle
* <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">NADPH</mark>: Helps add high energy electrons.
* These processes can be described as $$\text{ATP} + \text{NADPH} + 6\text{CO}_2 \to \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$$
