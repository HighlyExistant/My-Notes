# Letter 4
## Vocabulary
* ==**Emaciated**==: thinned out, made skinny.
## Plot
Again to Mrs. Saville, location unknown, 1 month later on August 5th, 17--. An accident occurred on July 31st, 14 days after their last letter. They were almost captured by the ice and the fog become thicker. Around 2 o'clock however it had cleared. They encountered a human being on a sledge alongside a single dog. The sailors tried to convince them to climb aboard, where they addressed them in "perfect English, although with a foreign accent". Their limbs were frozen, his body emaciated, fatigued and suffering. The stranger recovers, but they are filled with grief and are trying to find someone who abandoned them.

It is then, 8 days later on August 13th that they write that their affection for this stranger grows. "He is gentle yet wise. His mind is cultivated, and he speaks with great eloquence", says Robert. The stranger speaks about how he has lost everything and cant start life anew.

6 days later on August 19th, Robert says that the stranger told him "You may easily perceive, Captain, that I have suffered great misfortunes. I had determined at one time that the memory of these evils should die with me, but you have convinced me otherwise. You seek for knowledge and wisdom, as I once did. I hope that the gratification of your wishes may not be a serpent to sting you, as mine has been. I do not know if a description of my disasters will be useful to you, but I know this: you are pursuing the same course and exposing yourself to the same dangers that have made me what I am. Perhaps you may be able to deduce an apt moral from my tale.", the stranger waits for one event only now, what I presume to be death, and Robert wishes to jot these down in the strangers own words as well as they can.

# Chapter 2
## Definitions
* ==**vehement**==: intensely emotional, strong
## Characters
* ==**Henry Clerval**==: Someone Victor befriended, the son of a merchant in Geneva, who has an unusual talent and imagination, as well as loved enterprise. Loved books of chivalry and romance, as well as tales of knightly adventures, with such heroes as Roncesvalles and the Round Table of King Arthur, and the Crusades.
* ==**Victor Frankenstein**==: Ill/Violent Tempered, with ==**vehement**== passions. Loves the sciences and was fascinated to learn them.
## Plot
We can tell that Victor is a scientist at heart, who really wants to understand the laws of nature and the world he resides. Although it would seem that Victor would be depressed in their studies, Elizabeth was there to help them. Her, as well as Henry. The great loves of Victor were ""science, and natural philosophy".

When Victor was 13 years old, they took a trip near Thonon to some natural hot springs. As they were in the inn, he started reading "Cornelius Agrippa" a book authored around 1486-1535, of the occult, where his father then told him not to waste time on that trash. This only furthered their love of the occult, even if the contents of the books were already disproven by modern science. He became their disciple. He was guided by terrible disproven science.

When Victor was 15 years old, they witnessed a thunderstome advance from the mountains, and it took ahold of their curiosity. A man had visited their house, excited about the stump that had been electrocuted and Victor quickly forgot about Cornelius Agrippa, Albertus Magnus and Parcelus, as they began to look into electricity and mathematics. To Victor, this was a guardian angel guiding him.
# Chapter 3
## Characters
* ==**Dr. Krempe**==: Professor at the University of Ingolstadt, of Chemistry, to Victor.
* ==**Dr. Waldman**==: Another professor at the University of Ingolstadt, of Chemistry, which substitutes Dr. Krempe when they are not available. Appears as if they are 50 years old, has a few grey hairs, although his back hairs were black. He is short but stood straight, and has a very sweet voice.
* ==**Victor Frankenstein**==: The protagonist
## Plot
When Victor was 17 years old, he went to the university of Ingolstadt. Although he had attended schools in Geneva, his father wanted him to get accustomed to other cultures. Elizabeth had a severe scarlet fever, and while she managed to survive, their mother had caught the disease and it led to her demise. After witnessing this death, Victor was distraught.

Victor delayed his leave to University as he felt it was a sin to leave so soon after their mothers passing. Henry Clerval tried to convince his father to go to the same university as the protanist, but failed, as his father didnt see the point in it.

When Victor finally entered University Dr. Krempe tells them that the authors they had read on the occult, were useless, and he must learn everything again.

One day Dr. Waldman gives a lecture about the history of chemistry. Unlike Dr. Krempe, Waldman had a fascination for the old masters and thanked them for their work, as without them, we wouldn't be where we are now. Although he admits that their attempts at an elixir of life and transmuting metals was foolish, they are still thankful. We now know to Victors last name to be Frankenstein. He talks to themselves, convinced they will pioneer chemistry as a whole, and discover unknown powers.

Victor couldn't sleep and visited Waldman. He told the same stories he had to Dr. Krempe, to which Waldman had listened proudly. Waldman had left him a list of books to read, just like Dr. Krempe did, but this time Victor was more eager.
# Chapter 4
## Vocabulary
* ==**Charnel Houses**==: a building or vault in which corpses or bones are piled.
## Plot
Ever since their encounter with Dr. Waldman, Chemistry and natural philosophy is all they think about. They read the reccomendations of both Waldman and Krempe. For Victor, Waldman had guided their path towards knowledge, and while a slow learner, they came to understand more and more as time went on. His passion for science impressed both student and master, and for two years this was his life, not paying attention to his past in Geneva. He had even improved some chemical instruments, which led him to have more admiration in the university.

Of all the interesting subjects they encountered, the one that interested him the most was the structure and nature of the human body, and how they were endowed life. He learned about the human body, its natural decay, and spend several days and nights in charnel houses. After so long they had been struck with the secret to the cause of life, as well as developed the ability to bestow life on lifeless matter.

Victor now speaks to us directly in the 2nd person, saying that they will not tell us this secret, so we may not commit the same mistake they commited.

He spent months gathering materials in hopes of creating an 8 foot tall creature. As his passion increased his health decreased, as he became paler and lost weight. Through failed attempts they collected body parts from charnel houses, dissecting rooms and slaughter houses. He ignored family and friends.

> *No man should allow himself any pursuit that might interfere with the tranquility of his family life. If this rule had been steadily observed, Greece would not have been enslaved, Cæsar would have spared his country, America would have been discovered more gradually, and the empires of Mexico and Peru would not have been destroyed. But I digress. - Victor Frankenstein in Frankenstein by Mary Shelley*
# Chapter 5
## Characters
* ==**The Creature**==: It has dull yellow eyes, breathes hard. Victor chose his features to make him as beautiful as he could. His skin was yellow, with long black hair and pearly white teeth, horribly contrasted by his watery eyes. His lips were straight and black.
## Plot
His work was finally complete. He collected the instruments that would bring about life and sparked it into existence. And as they stared into their creation, Victor felt brief aw followed by intense disgust. Now with his dream accomplished, reality set in.

As they tried going to sleep, they dreamed that they had seen Elizabeth and had imprinted a kiss on her lips which then turned pale, as if now they held a corpse, and as her features changed in the dream, it was as if they held the corpse of their dead mother. As Victor has this nightmare the creature, perhaps trying to comfort stretches out his hand, but Victor runs in fear. He says "Oh, the face of that creature! No human being could look on it without horror!". 

> *He was ugly then, but when those muscles and joints were at last put into motion, he became a thing such as even Dante could not have conceived. - Victor Frankenstein in Frankenstein by Mary Shelley*

He leaves his house and as he walks in terror at what might be lurking, he spots a carriage carrying none other than Henry Clerval. He is happy to see his friend and so was Henry. After many months he finally felt calm. Henry talks again about how his father doesn't let him enter the University, as they both talk about their lives. As they speak Henry tells Victor how unhealthy they look, as they are now thin and pale. Henry asks if they have been busy, to which Frankenstein says they have been, although they seem avoidant on what it is exactly. 

Victor invited Henry to his place, with fear that the monster would still be there, but thankfully it wasn't. This made Victor laugh in such a way it was disturbing to Henry, in which Henry asks what is happening, in a worried tone. Victor feels pity for Henry, as their reunion which was supposed to be sweet has now turned bitter. He had obtained a fever to which Henry tended him for several months. It was due to his fear of the monster he had created, and while he did tell Henry of this monster, Henry didn't seem to take them in a literal sense. In time however, Victor did recover, and he thanked Henry. While it seems Henry does want to know about the creature, he stops himself as he doesn't want to discomfort Victor on that subject. Instead he asks Victor to write his family a letter, to assure them they are alright, since they have been silent for a long time now.

# Chapter 7
At the end of spring, Victor receives a letter from his father saying that William is dead. He was murdered. Ernest and William had gone to play hide and seek, when suddenly Ernest couldn't find William. They searched during nighttime but still couldn't find him. Later on they had found their motionless corpse stretched on the grass, pale and with the marks of the murderers finger son his neck. Elizabeth blames herself for Williams death because they gifted William a necklace, which she thinks attracted the killer. Victors father now pleads for Victor to return and console Elizabeth.

> *Come, Victor; with thoughts of revenge against the assassin, but with feelings of peace and gentleness that will heal the wounds of our minds. Enter the house of mourning. But I would have you do so with kindness and affection for those who love you, and not with hatred for your enemies. - Victors father in Frankenstein by Mary Shelley*

The letter was written in May 12th, 17--.

When Victor reached Geneva, it was completely dark. They were forced to spend the night at a village, Secheron. He was restless and decided to go for a walk when he saw the creature! Victor was afraid and trying to figure out why this creature was in his hometown, and if it was he who killed their brother William. It's been 2 years now since Victor created the monster, and he wondered if this was his first crime.

Victor wanted to tell his family of this news, but realized that he would sound insane if he told that to his family:

> *I would have to tell: a being that I myself had created and given life had traveled from Ingolstadt to Geneva, killed my beloved brother and then met me at midnight in a storm. I knew that if any other person told me such a story, I would look upon it as the crazy talk of a madman. And of course I had only recently recovered from months of sickness and delirium. After a few minutes of thinking, I convinced myself that it would be better to remain silent. - Victor in Frankenstein by Mary Shelley*

They have found the murderer, and it appears to be Justine Moritz!? Victor is shocked at this accusation, he assumes she must have been framed since he knows that it was the creature who committed the crime. Victor tells them that it can't be her, and that how can anyone believe she did it, to which they respond:

> *No one did at first, but several details have come to light that have almost forced us to believe it. Her behavior has been very strange and confused. But she will be tried today, and you will be able to hear all that has been learned. - Ernest in  Frankenstein by Mary Shelley*

Justine is to be tried this morning.
