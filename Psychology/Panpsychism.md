The belief that the mind or consciousness is a fundamental feature of reality, which exists throughout the universe. Given its name by philosopher Francesco Patrizi. It's not necessarily a theory of the mind, as much as it is a [[Rhetoric#^f14de4|conjecture]]. It has generally been regarded by most philosophers as too fantastical to be true.
* It is mutually exclusive with [[Emergentism|emergentism]] which suggests that it is an emergent property.
The arguments for panpsychism suggest that consciousness is either caused by some sort of panpsychism or emergentism.
1. Epicurus: *the mental quality called will could not arise from non-will, and therefore that the atoms from which everything was made had to possess a kind of will themselves. Will cannot emerge ex nihilo, and thus is present in the very constituents of matter.*$^{\text{(1)}}$
2. Telesio: *nothing can give what it does not possess*.$^{\text{(1)}}$

> *The basic problem is this: emergence seems, at first glance, to be a reasonable enough idea, but when pressed for details it comes up sorely lacking. In fact, emergence of mind is very difficult to sensibly explain. Mind is not like five-fingered-ness, or warm-bloodedness. These things, which clearly did emerge, are ontologically unlike mind. They are simply reconfigurations of existing physical matter, whereas mind is of a different ontological order. It is too fundamental an aspect of existence to be comparable to ordinary biological structural features.*$^{\text{(1)}}$

In some sense they aren't necessarily arguing that emergentism doesn't exist, but that there is a fundamental thing that causes this to take form. 

Some panpsychists disagree on whether everything absolutely has some level of consciousness, or just certain things. So the pan here meaning all wouldn't apply to them in a sense, although they generally hold the same beliefs. This should not be confused with ==**Property dualism**==, which suggests that physical substance is split into two categories, either physical or mental.
