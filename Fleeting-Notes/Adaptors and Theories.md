An adaptor is a connection between two different means which allow them to be used interchangeably. A transitive relation where you can reach one end through the composition of various other relations. In the context of theories, where one comes up with a possible explanation as to why something is the way that it is, through the formulation of assumptions which help us deduce other things, there can be a wide array of theories that can help us accomplish the same things. I take with me a very structuralist view of theories, and indeed of various things. I do believe that the whole can be explained through the composition of various smaller wholes. It is for that reason that when we bring about a new theory $T$, we can come up with various adaptors towards that theory, known as $T'$ which lead us to an explanation of why theory $T$ works, while also giving us a fuller explanation through the means of theory $T'$. It is important however that the explanations provided in the theory $T$ are inside of $T'$, that is $T \subseteq T'$. Within them there are also the different formulations of the theory $T$ which are in effect other ways of viewing the same information. These different formulations can be seen as equivalent if you can derive one from the other.

# Proof being the basis of trust
There are layers to provability and trustworthiness of information. Ranging from values that are always false or true, Sometimes false and Sometimes true, as well as whether they are provably false or true we are given:
1. Contradiction: Is a statement that is always false, through an inherent contradiction with the information given. Giving an example: *All humans are smart*, *Fulano is a human*, *Fulano is not smart*. This forms a contradiction, since if all humans are smart, and Fulano is a human, then it should follow that Fulano is smart. It is important to note here, that we should also watch out for generalizations when forming our statements like the fact we said that *all humans are smart*, is quite a leap, as well as what exactly we mean by *smart*. Here we can find the ***generalization***. These are claims that, while not entirely false, are also not entirely true, due to how general the statement is. For example, *all triangles are purple*, cannot be true for all humans or all triangles, as only some triangles are purple. It is then better to say some triangles *some triangles are purple*, than *all triangles are purple*, as it brings it back to the realm of possibility. That is not to say that every statement that uses *all* is a generalization, as it is true that all triangles do possess 3 sides, but that is by definition. A generalization then, is formed by a [[Critique of Pure Reason#^9b7ce9|synthetic judgement]].
2. Unproven Statements: These are statements which are not supported by theory or empirical evidence. They can generally be disregarded using Hitchens Razor.
3. Subjective Statements: These are statements with which their truth value depends on the subject asked. For example, *all humans are smart*, can be true or false depending on the person or the context. If we talk about smart as in, "knowing math", then not every human knows math. If we talk about being smart as in, "being a rational being", then some would say that yes all humans are rational beings. While some would say that this definition does not apply to people who have no brain activity, others could say that since those people are functionally dead, they cannot be held to the same regard as a human being. To some extent these statements are subjective and dependent on context.
4. Conjecture: These are statements that appear to be true, but have no underlying proof as to be certain they are true. That is not to say that since there is no proof, the statement is false, but that if some other statement comes up which can explain the conjecture, then that one should be taken instead. For example, if I said that there existed invisible unknowable hands that controlled all the forces in the universe, and that is why things move about, well the invisible, unknowable hands cannot be measured, and thus cannot be proven, and yet there is already a much better explanation that can be proven as to why there are forces in the world, such as electrons, protons, neutrons and mass. One could argue that it is the invisible hands which these particles possess that cause these changes, but the explanations aren't too useful when trying to predict things, and can, through the use of philosophical razors (in this case Hitchens razor), be discarded as a good way of viewing the universe.
5. Theories and Laws: Are things that through empirical observation, have been formulated to predict future phenomena. While these statements are up for discussion, they have been proven time and time again. In some sense however, you could also say these fall under some sort of conjecture, but this time it can be proven, as it is measurable.
6. Tautology: These are statements that are true by definition, through some [[Critique of Pure Reason#^a1be38|analytic judgement]].
It is through this ladder of trustworthiness that we can see some truths being stronger than others. 
# Adaptors
Occasionally there are ways to reach the same conclusion using two schools of thought. We can make a conclusion, and justify it using a different set of beliefs. The set of beliefs would be called the adaptor, as it is interchangeable in reaching the conclusion. That is to say that when making a conclusion, the two sets of beliefs can be viewed as interchangeable. We can present this as a mathematical relation, where two sets of beliefs are equivalent if they can reach a similar set of conclusions. It is important to note that the two sets must be able to reach all the elements in the set of conclusions, and we cant just arbitrarily add conclusions.
