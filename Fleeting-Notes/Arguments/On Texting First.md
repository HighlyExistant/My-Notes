I'm often prevented from texting first for fear of various different arguments I make in my head. I know these arguments are of course fallacious, but even with that knowledge, they still creep into the back of my head. Instead of living with the sorrow of loneliness and wanting to talk to someone when I feel like I can't, I will instead argue against these thoughts, and cover every edge case I can.
# I Have Nothing to Say
I'd say there exists 2 types of texts:
1. Contextual Texts: Texts that take place in the middle of already texting. They have context present from the previous texts that were sent. These are texts that you can easily respond to, and that usually means there already has been something said.
2. Non contextual Texts: These texts require no context. These are usually the first texts sent to someone, before the start of a conversation.
Our problem lies in the second category, where we require the writing of a text, with zero context, to hopefully strike up a conversation, or not. These texts, if I were to list them ostensibly, would be: 
* A video, picture or any other piece of media.
* A "Good morning" or "Goodnight" text, or any text alike.
* "How was your day", "How's your life going"
* "Do you wanna do X?"
As you can see, even if these are very simple texts, they can serve as starting points.
## What will I even say if my life isn't interesting?
If you have nothing to say, then what will you say if you truly do have nothing to talk about? Well you can try talking about 
* A TV Show or Movie you've been watching/already watched, and recommend it to that individual.
* Try talking about your special interests, and see if they enjoy it too.
* Plan an activity, if you don't have anything interesting to say, why not make something interesting
# They don't like me enough to text back
There can be various other reasons why someone doesn't text back, such as:
* They are really busy.
* They feel bad and don't want to text as of right now.
* They're waiting for you to text them first. They ask themselves the same questions as this list.
These of course don't apply if you aren't even friends with this person, or if you truly did do something to make them not like you.
# I've been texting a lot, I don't want to seem clingy
You don't need to text all the time if you don't feel like it of course. In this situation it seems as though the problem isn't whether you should text first, but that you've been texting first too much. In this case I'd recommend you take a break. You don't need to talk to anyone if you don't feel like it.
# I feel bad, I don't think I can text
Out of every time I have texted a friend, while I felt bad, I usually felt better. Of course, I have good friends, and if you don't have that, then you should consider getting new friends.
# They haven't been responding
If this is the case, evaluate whether or not this is because they are busy, and think back to whether or not they have something planned for that day. If they don't have something planned for that day, you can assume they are tired, and you should wait, in which case you can text 5 hours later (unless it falls between a period of time they are surely sleeping). If they still don't answer, then you should space your texts at least every Fibonacci sequence, starting in the 6th iteration, therefore it will go: $$5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55, 89, 144, \text{...}$$ this resets when they text back.
