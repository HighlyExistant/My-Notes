## <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Principle of Impartiality</mark>
Given a set of rules $R$ proposed by any individual $i\in P$ then 
    $\forall p\in P$ the rules $R$ apply
This rule is supposed to get rid of hypocrisy and contradictions. It is also supposed to imply Hitchens Razor.
### Example
* Let Jessie be an individual.
* Jessie proposes that stealing is wrong.
* Jessie steals from their friend.
* In this case, Jessie is in the wrong for breaking their own rule.
### Problems Section
1. This is under the assumption that all partiality is bad, which it is not necessarily so.
##  <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Principle of Consequence</mark>
For every dilema there exists a reasoning for a solution. Let $S$ be the solution to a dilema $D$ from a family of similar dilemas $\mathbf{D}$, then $\forall D\in\mathbf{D}\Rightarrow S$ is a valid solution.

It basically states that based on a previous dilema, another dilema can be answered if the criteria of reasoning is met.
### Problems Section
1.  Some added context can keep the integrity of the main dilemma and yet still, understandably, change a persons solution. Ex:
     If petra does not know that jessie is a rapist, and that forms a family of similar dilemmas, there can be one where she has been offered to converse about jessies unspecific wrong doings yet she has refused. petra indeed does not know, but because of her own refusal to seek out information
### Possible Solutions Section
1. The related dilemas $\mathbf{D}$ would need to be adjusted for added contexts. More specifically, this principle can be defined as a form of universal prescriptivism, and therefore falls into the very same pitfalls. To make my prescriptivist theory plausible, it would need to stand against emotivism and quasi-realism. Ultimately however, the true problem is defining at what point is a dilema similar enough to be categorized in the family of dilemas. However, a downfall to this, and similar categorizations is such: "a racist "fanatic" advocating the deportation of all minority-group members, while maintaining consistency (even if the racist is a member of the minority group), cannot be criticized for either irrationality or falsehood"
# <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Ought implies Can</mark>
Given an action $A$ and a situation $S$ whether you ought to apply $A$ depends on whether you can.

In this context, the word ==**can**== applies not only to whether you can physically do something, but also whether you are mentally capable of doing said thing. If you are mentally incapable of performing an action, then it is equivalent to being physically incapable of doing said action.

### Zugzwang Formulation
This principle can override any other principle as long as it can be proven that you had no other viable choices (your freedom to choose was completely hindered / every move was a bad move).
