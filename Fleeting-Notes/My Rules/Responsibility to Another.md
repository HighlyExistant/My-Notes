As we take on **promises** we gain more responsibility to other people. Here I take a deontological approach to responsibility.
# Article 1. Definitions
Definitions of the following words in this document will be:
1. ==**Promise**==: Self inflicted responsibility for future action.
2. ==**Kept promise**==: A promise that has been accomplished fully.
3. ==**Unkept promise**==: A promise that has been completely disregarded.
4. ==**Ongoing promise**==: A promise that, while not fully complete, is on its way to being fully accomplished.
5. ==**Abandoned promise**==: A promise that, while partially complete, has now been abandoned.
6. ==**Broken promise**==: A promise which was contradicted. A promise who has had their principles violated.
7. ==**Indefinite promise**==: A promise that does not have a completion requirement, and must instead be done until either it is abandoned, broken or relieved.
8. ==**Trust**==: Capacity for well performed action. In terms of promises, the trust you have that someone will enact a promise, is given by the ratio of kept and unkept promises.
9. ==**Prefixed time**==: Time at which a task should be started.
10. ==**Deadline**==: Time at which a task should be finished.
# Article 2. Promises 
## Section 1. Making Promises
A promise is made through the use of performative language, which insinuates that the subject in question will enact said action. This can be through 
1. The use of any personal pronoun or noun relating to yourself, which is paired with the action ([[Linguistics/Grammar/Syntax/The Basics#^641e52|predicate]]) that will be performed.
2. Agreeing to the terms and conditions of another which relates to you accomplishing a task. 
All promises must be done consensually as of postulate 2, and if a promise violates the postulates set by the rules from which these are derived, they can be broken without remorse.
## Section 2. Status of Promises
A promise has 4 statuses relating to how far along a promise is in. which is *kept*, *unkept*, *ongoing* and *abandoned*. 

There are then the temporal statuses of promises which would be classified as *early*, *on time* and *late*. If something is early, it means that there was a prefixed time at which a task should've been started, but was instead finished ahead of that time period. Not all tasks have a prefixed time, as the time a task is given, is usually when the promise becomes ongoing. These are then reserved for when a task was finished before being assigned, or when an explicit time to start was given and was accomplished beforehand. An on time task, is then a task that was completed between the prefixed time and the deadline. This is usually where you want to finish said tasks. If a task is completed after the deadline, it becomes a late task. Not all tasks have deadlines, in which case these are called ==**indefinite promises**==.

The importance of a promise is relative to the people making the promise, unless explicitly stated. That said, one should assume how important a task is, based on how much it will affect people, either mentally, socially or financially. If you break a promise, and it can be proven that the broken promise was a cause for any inflicted damage, then you are partially at fault as of postulate 3 and 4.
# Article 3. Relationships
## Section 1. The Contract
Every relation between two individuals is upheld by a contract, either implicit or explicit, on how both parties behave towards one another. It is fair to have an assumption of ones relation towards another, but it is only when explicitly stated that this can become true. 
## Section 2. Status of Relationships
A relationship will have $X$ categories which are listed as follows.
1. Familiarity: How much you know about the person, and how you regard them. This ranges as the following
	* Stranger: This is to say you have no familiarity with the person, and have most likely never interacted or heard of the person before. More importantly, you lack their **name**.
	* Familiar: After the acquisition of personal information, you are now in some way familiar with the individual. You have their name, and possibly even a means to communicate.
	* Acquaintance: These are people you regularly interact with, either through the work place or by purposefully meeting with them. The key difference between an acquaintance and a friend, is that your relationship has not been explicitly stated.
2. Feelings: How do you feel towards the particular individual. This is not more of a ranking, as emotions are complicated, so it can be categorized more as a tuple of emotions each ranging from a complete lack of said emotion, to fully enveloped in it. Something to note about emotions is that they are ephemeral, and so usually assigning a number towards someone on how you feel towards them can be hard. Instead then, there should be 2 separate rankings. One should serve as the total average of emotion you feel during a specific time period, and the other should be how you feel in the current moment.
3. Assertions: This is when something is explicitly stated as such. This is how you want the relation to be viewed, and as such, is something you should not lie about. When it comes to these assertions, they should be given with **consent**, and without motive to deceive.
4. Legality: What is the current legal status of the relationship. Unlike the others, this is an objective fact.
