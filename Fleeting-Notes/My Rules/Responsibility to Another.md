As we take on **promises** we gain more responsibility to other people. Here I take a deontological approach to responsibility.
# Section 1. Definitions
Definitions of the following words in this document will be:
1. ==**Promise**==: Self inflicted responsibility for future action.
2. ==**Kept promise**==: A promise that has been accomplished fully.
3. ==**Unkept promise**==: A promise that has been completely disregarded.
4. ==**Ongoing promise**==: A promise that, while not fully complete, is on its way to being fully accomplished.
5. ==**Abandoned promise**==: A promise that, while partially complete, has now been abandoned.
6. ==**Broken promise**==: A promise which was contradicted. A promise who has had their principles violated.
7. ==**Indefinite promise**==: A promise that does not have a completion requirement, and must instead be done until either it is abandoned, broken or relieved.
8. ==**Trust**==: Capacity for well performed action. In terms of promises, the trust you have that someone will enact a promise, is given by the ratio of kept and unkept promises.
9. ==**Prefixed time**==: Time at which a task should be started.
10. ==**Deadline**==: Time at which a task should be finished.
11. ==**Morally Corrupt**==: Dubious morality which is prone to violate the rights of others. While it applies to all the principles, here it emphasizes more the 2nd principle.
# Section 2. Promises 
## Subsection 1. Making Promises
It is hereby stated that a promise is made through the use of performative language, which insinuates that the subject in question will enact said action. This can be through 
1. The use of any personal pronoun or noun relating to yourself, which is paired with the action ([[Linguistics/Grammar/Syntax/The Basics#^641e52|predicate]]) that will be performed.
2. Agreeing to the terms and conditions of another which relates to you accomplishing a task.
Wherein all promises must be done consensually as of principle 2. If a promise violates the principles set by the rules from which these are derived, they can be broken without remorse.
## Subsection 2. Status of Promises
A promise will have 4 statuses relating to how far along a promise is to completion, as well as whether the promise is in active progress. These are listed as *kept*, *unkept*, *ongoing* and *abandoned*. 

The temporal statuses of promises will be classified as *early*, *on time* and *late*. If something is early, it means that there was a prefixed time at which a task should've been started, but was instead finished ahead of that time period. Not all tasks have a prefixed time, as the time a task is given, is usually when the promise becomes ongoing. These are then reserved for when a task was finished before being assigned, or when an explicit time to start was given and was accomplished beforehand. An on time task, is then a task that was completed between the prefixed time and the deadline. This is usually where you want to finish said tasks. If a task is completed after the deadline, it becomes a late task. Not all tasks have deadlines, in which case these are called ==**indefinite promises**==.
## Subsection 3. Importance of a Task and or Promise
Some promises are more important than others. The importance of a promise is relative to the people making the promise, unless explicitly stated. One may assume how important a task is, based on how it will affect others, either mentally, socially or financially. If a promise is broken, and it can be proven that the broken promise was a cause for any inflicted damage, then you are partially at fault as of principle 3., and it is for that case that they should be given a specific importance ranking.
1. ==**Inconsequential**==. These are promises that, if broken, have no external consequences or effects to anyone. These promises can generally be completely disregarded, as they have no negative, or positive effects, on anyone.
2. ==**Consequential**==. There are two types of consequential promises which are
	1. ==**External Consequential**==: Those which affect others. These promises should be kept, as they can affect the lives of others, especially if the consequence for not keeping the promise is negative. Of course this can be overruled if evidence that it is impossible to be kept via principle 3, or if it violates any of the other principles.
	2. ==**Internal Consequential**==: Those which affect solely yourself. These promises can be weighed using your judgement, as they would only affect yourself. The moment it affects another it becomes an external consequential.
# Section 3. Relationships
## Subsection 1. The Contract
Every relation between two individuals is upheld by a contract, either implicit or explicit, on how both parties behave towards one another. One can form an assumption on ones relation with another based on inductive reasoning, but it is only when explicitly stated that this relation can be verified.
## Subsection 2. Status of Relationships
A relationship will have 4 categories which are listed as follows.
1. Familiarity: How much you know about the person, and how you regard them. This ranges as the following
	* Stranger: This is to say you have no familiarity with the person, and have most likely never interacted or heard of the person before. More importantly, you lack their **name**.
	* Familiar: After the acquisition of personal information, you are now in some way familiar with the individual. You have their name, and possibly even a means to communicate.
	* Acquaintance: These are people you regularly interact with, either through the work place or by purposefully meeting with them. The key difference between an acquaintance and a friend, is that your relationship has not been explicitly stated.
2. Feelings: How do you feel towards the particular individual. This is not more of a ranking, as emotions are complicated, so it can be categorized more as a tuple of emotions each ranging from a complete lack of said emotion, to fully enveloped in it. Something to note about emotions is that they are ephemeral, and so usually assigning a number towards someone on how you feel towards them can be hard. Instead then, there should be 2 separate rankings. One should serve as the total average of emotion you feel during a specific time period, and the other should be how you feel in the current moment.
3. Assertions: This is when something is explicitly stated as such. This is how you want the relation to be viewed, and as such, is something you should not lie about. When it comes to these assertions, they should be given with **consent**, and without motive to deceive.
4. Legality: What is the current legal status of the relationship. Unlike the others, this is an objective fact.
## Subsection 3. Consent
For consent to be present you need the following things
1. Decisionality: The capacity for one to make decisions, such as a medical condition, or being intoxicated can affect this. While one can still have some level of Decisionality, even under these things, the more important a decision, the more capacity for thought one should have. Another thing that can impede decisionality is information, meaning that any act of omission which purposefully misrepresents the task at hand, would fall under a breach of decisionality. It should also be noted that any serious threat made to another would also impede their decisionality, as it is noted as coercion.
2. Clear Affirmation: After decisionality has been obtained, you then need a clear signal that denotes a *yes*.
# Section 4. Belief
## Subsection 1. In another's words
We cannot know everything in this world, as it is too vast to fully comprehend, and so we often take people at their word. This is not wrong, but it is still dangerous to believe everything another tells you. It could even happen that you spread something someone said, that is completely not true. The status of whether to evaluate truth or not, depends on how objective that truth is, which I categorize in [[Adaptors and Theories|Adaptors and Theories]].
* Contradictions should be immediately ruled out, and replaced with a correct possible explanation.
* Unproven statements should be disregarded if they are wrong, or evaluated if they fall into conjecture.
* Theories and Laws should only be evaluated if they are proven wrong through some edge case.
* Tautologies should not be questioned, as they are correct by definition.
Out of all of these statements presented however, the most likely to get passed around via rumors are unproven statements. Due to the lack of evidence for or against this, it can be easy to dismiss, but in the case of serious allegations, such as of someone being morally corrupt, then it should be evaluated. ==**In this case it should be looked at through[[On Induction|induction]]**==.
