## Principles
These are rules that will be followed throughout the writing of my rules. It should be noted that if you believe in moral relativism, then these principles can be viewed pragmatically.
1. ==**For every rule proposed towards others, one is not exempt from that rule.**== This is to say that any logic one uses in argument, cannot assume the opposite for oneself. If I say, "Thou shalt not kill", then I am also forbidden from doing it. Same goes with the logic of my statements, if I make an assumption towards one thing, it is only fair for someone to use the same process of judgement towards their assumptions. ==**Similarly, it functions as Hitchens' razor where**==, "That which can be asserted without evidence can be dismissed without evidence". This postulate is supposed to get rid of any contradictions that may arise in argument, although it does not get rid of any fallacious exemptions we make towards certain things.
2. ==**"The liberty of one citizen ends where the liberty of another citizen begins" - Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes**==. This makes it so that you cannot impose your will on other people (rape, murder, etc). It also ==**gives people freedom of choice for themselves, but not for others**==. This also acts as a weaker version of the golden rule where ==**you will treat others how you want to be treated.**== While one could strawman the destruction of prisons using this postulate, ==**I add here an exception where those who are a danger towards others (take away the freedom of others), should be subject to lesser or equal punishment**==.
3. ==**When accepting the duty to protect another, you are responsible for anything that befalls the one you protect.**== 
4. ==**When delegating someone with the duty to protect another, you are more than or equally as responsible for the one you entrust and the one that has been entrusted to protect.**==
5. ==**For every two words and definitions which describe the same thing, it should be treated as if they are both the very same thing.**== There is often a need to disguise things in language, by phrasing things in ways that make them seem more palatable. When something is obfuscated by buzzwords and incoherent language games, the words should be questioned and translated into what they truly represent, with all the implications and relations laid out. ==**This can be used to criticize things like the prison industrial complex, and its similarities to slavery**==.
6. ==**Act in such a way as to treat humanity, whether in your own person or in that of anyone else, always as an end and never merely as a means.**== This is just Kant's second formulation of the categorical imperative. Out of all the formulations of the categorical imperative, this is by far my favorite, as it provides a golden rule which doesn't suggest an obligation of action, but also doesn't limit the necessity we have towards needing help. As long as humans are viewed as humans, then this is what is to be good. It makes it so that bigotry does not prevail.
## Definitions
* ==**Rule**==: *Set of explicit regulations or principles to govern within*. Derivatives of rules are:
	* ==**Restrictions**==: Set of things one shall not do.
	* ==**Suggestions**==: Set of things one is recommended to do or not do.
	* ==**Obligations**==: Set of things one must do.
* ==**Exceptions**==: Every rule is nuanced and complex, and therefore some rules may have exceptions.
