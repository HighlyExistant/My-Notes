## Principles
These are rules that will be followed throughout the writing of my rules. It should be noted that if you believe in moral relativism, then these principles can be viewed pragmatically. While I provide some examples for each principle, that does not mean that is their only purpose.
1. ==**For every rule proposed towards others, one is not exempt from that rule.**== If we were to express this in terms of logic, it is to say that any rule, whether it be in argument or in law and ethics, it should also be applicable to oneself. This is to decry hypocrisy, and make it clear that the weapons of your opponents can also be used against them. Same goes with the logic of my statements, if I make an assumption towards one thing, it is only fair for someone to use the same process of judgement towards their assumptions. ==**Similarly, Hitchens' razor can be derived from this statement**==, "That which can be asserted without evidence can be dismissed without evidence". 
2. ==**"The liberty of one citizen ends where the liberty of another citizen begins" - Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes**==. This makes it so that you cannot impose your will on other people (rape, murder, etc). It also ==**gives people freedom of choice for themselves, but not for others**==. This also acts as a weaker version of the golden rule where ==**you will treat others how you want to be treated.**== While one could strawman the destruction of prisons using this postulate, ==**I add here an exception where those who are a danger towards others (take away the freedom of others), should be subject to lesser or equal punishment**==.
3. ==**Ought implies can**==:  This states that you are only morally required to do things that you are able to do. This can be, for example, not stepping in to stop a robber, since you aren't able to defend against them anyways, and will just put yourself in danger. It also says that you shouldn't be blamed for things that are outside your control. This principle can override any other principle, as long as you have no other viable choices.
4. ==**For every two words and definitions which describe the same thing, it should be treated as if they are both the very same thing.**== There is often a need to disguise things in language, by phrasing things in ways that make them seem more palatable. When something is obfuscated by buzzwords and incoherent language games, the words should be questioned and translated into what they truly represent, with all the implications and relations laid out. ==**This can be used to criticize things like the prison industrial complex, and its similarities to slavery**==.
5. ==**Act in such a way as to treat humanity, whether in your own person or in that of anyone else, always as an end and never merely as a means.**== This is just Kant's second formulation of the categorical imperative. Out of all the formulations of the categorical imperative, this is by far my favorite, as it provides a golden rule which doesn't suggest an obligation of action, but also doesn't limit the necessity we have towards needing help. As long as humans are viewed as humans, then this is what is to be good. It makes it so that bigotry does not prevail.
6. ==**Active denial implies willing ignorance**==. This is for people who choose to hide behind a veil of ignorance to justify their actions, or choose not to hear bad things just to stay in their own ignorant bubble. This is of course a vice, because while ignorance can grant you bliss, I am personally not a hedonist, and don't think anyone should strive for hedonism.
7. ==**Actions speak louder than words**==. To say is easy, but praxis is necessary for truly achieving knowledge of good. You can know the right decision to make, but if you don't make it, then your knowledge is meaningless. Therefore, it is justifiable to lose trust in someone if their words consistently contradict their actions. It's important to note here too, that the use of the word, "words", in this phrase does not necessarily mean spoken or written, but instead for any form of communication.
## Definitions
* ==**Rule**==: *Set of explicit regulations or principles to govern within*. Derivatives of rules are:
	* ==**Restrictions**==: Set of things one shall not do.
	* ==**Suggestions**==: Set of things one is recommended to do or not do.
	* ==**Obligations**==: Set of things one must do.
* ==**Exceptions**==: Every rule is nuanced and complex, and therefore some rules may have exceptions.

# The Rights of Humans
Everyone is entitled to:
1. Education: When speaking of education in the modern day, it means to be able to go to a school from Kindergarten to 12th grade, as well as having the opportunity to go study whatever one wishes in university.
2. Food: Access to healthy oils, greens (vegetables and fruit), whole grains, protein and vitamins.
3. Water: Access to clean drinkable water.
4. Shelter: Access to a home with protection against the weather, containing a place to sleep, shower and use of the toilet.
5. Travel: Access to visit the world from your base location. (this includes the access to migration and asylum).
