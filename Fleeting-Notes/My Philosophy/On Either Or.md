When given the argument on why it is impossible to do a completely good act, seeing as though _doing an act for the express reason of feeling good whilst doing that act is inherently selfish_, we lose track of what it even means to be _good_ or _bad_. Here I would propose that if such an action were to occur, one where you act for the pleasure of another, to further the pleasure of yourself, while one could say this act is selfish, it is not **bad**. If anything, looking at this from a perspective which maximizes happiness, we can see that not only have both parties been satisfied, no party was harmed in the process. So why is it that people see selfishness as a vice, regardless of context? To another extent selflessness is seen as a completely virtuous act, free of any such malintent usually associated with selfishness, even if such selflessness occasionally leads to the suffering of yourself.

I do not wish to promote hedonism, that is not the idea I am trying to plant, instead it is the idea that something is not _either or_, _black and white_. I will refer to these ideas, which are unquestioned contrasts as *false dichotomies*.
