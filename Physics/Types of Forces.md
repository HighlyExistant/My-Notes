### Conservative Forces
Is a function of position alone, where the energy is dependent on the starting and ending points, not the path taken. Changes in potential energy equals the work done against a conservative force, that is $\Delta PE=-W_c$. An example of a conservative force can be gravity $g$. In conservative forces we have kinetic energy $KE$ sometimes also referred to as $T$ and potential energy $PE$ sometimes referred to as $U$ or even $V$. The sum of of the function of these energies over time should give us a constant, due to the fact that the energy in a conserved system does not change, it just gets transformed. 
### Non-conservative Forces
Are forces that may add or remove energy from the system. Examples of this could be friction which dissipates energy into other forms, like heat.
