# What is it?
This is the concept that to produce something, we sacrifice production on other aspects. E.g. if there are 10 people, and all of them work on farming bread, then you will get 10 breads. But if you dedicate 2 people to making 1 cake instead and 8 people to bread, then you will make 8 bread and 1 cake. This means you will sacrifice 2 bread for every cake you might want to make. The reason why these costs don't have linearity, is because resources are not exactly strictly interchangeable.
# How to Represent it?
Given two variables "Capital Goods" and "Consumer Goods", you are given a set of possible production opportunities. The frontier can be thought of as the boundary of this set. The exterior of the set is not possible, while the [[Topology#Interior of a Set|interior]] is inefficient. Therefore the [[Topology#Boundary|boundary]], or the frontier, is the most efficient way of using resources, given what you have. To achieve the boundary, we must then maximize our use of resources.
