# Demand
Demand is a curve representing the quantity of goods and services that consumers are willing or able to buy, during some specific time.
## Efecto Sustitución
Between two products $p,q$ with the same function but different prices, people will tend to go for the product with the lowest price.
## Efecto Ingreso
When prices increase, consumers spend less, leaving them with more money. This will then lead them to spend more on normal goods.
### Inferior Goods
These are the goods you consume less of when you have a larger income.
## Determinants of Demand
For some product $X$, the demand for that product depends on:
1. Price of related products (substitution price)
2. Expectations of change in prices
3. Income of consumers
4. Consumer preference
5. Number of consumers
# Supply
## Determinants of Supply
For some product $X$, the supply of that product depends on:
1. Price of inputs in production
2. Price of other goods
3. Technology
4. Expectations of prices
5. Taxes
6. Number of producers
# Equilibrium of the Market
Let $E=(P^*, Q^*)$ be the point at the intersection of the supply line and demand line, then $P^*$ is known as the equilibrium price and $Q^*$ is the equilibrium supply, and $E$ is known as the point of equilibrium.

Let $A, B$ be arbitrary points which intersect at price $P'$, in the supply and demand line respectively, where $P'> P^*$. Then the distance $d(A,B)$ is the excess of production which will provoke the price to fall to $P^*$.

Let $C, D$ be arbitrary points which intersect at price $P''$, in the supply and demand line respectively, where $P''< P^*$. Then the distance $d(A,B)$ is the shortage of production which will provoke the price to rise to $P^*$.
