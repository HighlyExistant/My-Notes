Tells us the speed at which a consumer will respond in response to a change price in product of a product. ==**It does not tell us how large the magnitude of change is**==. 
# Formulas
The elasticity of demand can be represented as: $$\frac{\Delta Q/Q}{\Delta P/P}=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P}\cdot\frac{P}{Q}$$
It can be looked at as the percentage of the change in supply divided by the percentage of the change in demand.
# Determinantes de Elasticidad
1. Numero de sustitutos cercanos que tiene el producto.
	* Mas sustitutos $\Rightarrow$ mas elasticidad
2. Bien de lujo o de necesidad.
	* Es necesidad $\Rightarrow$ menos elasticidad
3. Proporción de gasto en el presupuesto total.
# Interpretations
Let $\epsilon_I$ represent the elasticity. 
* If $\epsilon_I > 1$ then it is a luxury good and considered elastic
* If $0<\epsilon_I<1$ then it is a good of normal necessity
* if $\epsilon_I<0$ it is an inferior good.
Things that are inelastic are typically necessities, which are hard to replace, e.g. medicine, electricity, water.
# Crossed Elasticity
This is defined as the percentage change of demand in a product $x$ over the percentage change in the price of some good $y$. $$\frac{Q_{x2}-Q_{x1}}{Q_{x1}}\cdot\frac{P_{y1}}{P_{y2}-P_{y1}}=\frac{\Delta Q_x}{Q_{x1}}\cdot\frac{P_{y1}}{\Delta P_y}$$
