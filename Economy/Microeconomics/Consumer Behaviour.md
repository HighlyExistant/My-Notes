# Utility
The pleasure derived from some good or service. We define this utility function for two commodities as: $$\mu (x,y)=x^{\frac{1}{2}}y^{\frac{1}{2}}$$such that $x,y$ are two goods or services. The curve formed by $\mu(x,y)=k$ represents the curve of indifference, where the pleasure derived from the different permutational quantities of $x,y$ are the same.
## Marginal Utility
It is the change of units of one good, per unit of another. Given two goods $x,y$ then the marginal utility would be: $$\frac{dy}{dx}$$ or the derivative.
### Law of Decreasing Marginal Utility
States that the more a product is consumed, the lesser the utility will be.
# Budget Line
Given some budget $B$ and two products with price $P_x$ and $P_y$ and the quantity of each product bought $Q_x$ and $Q_y$ 
 then the budget line would be, $$B=Q_x P_x+Q_y P_y$$The unknowns here are the quantities $Q_x$ and $Q_y$, and we're given a line of all possible quantities that can be bought of each given product. We can reorganize this as: $$Q_y=\frac{B-Q_xP_x}{P_y}$$The domain of the function would be $(0,\frac{B}{P_x})$.
## Maximizing on Utility
When $\frac{\mu M_x}{P_x}=\frac{\mu M_y}{P_y}$, where $M_x$ and $M_y$ is the marginal of the products, then we have maximized our budget on utility. Rephrased differently, its when the budget lines derivative is equal to the utilities marginal derivative.
