* ==**Input prices**==: Correspond to the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">cost of various factors of production</mark>.
* ==**Output prices**==: Refers to the <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">prices of the final products bought by consumers.</mark>
* ==**Short Run**==: How prices change in a short span of time. This leads to ==**input prices being fixed in the short run and output prices being flexible**==.
* ==**Long Run**==: How prices change in a long span of time. This leads to ==**input and output prices being flexible**==.
* ==**Full Employment**==: Potential output, "all available workers who want to work are employed". ==**In the future it is possible for a negative or positive GDP gap to occur**==.
* <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Aggregate Demand</mark>: Equals the sum of **Consumption**, **Investment**, **Government Spending** and **Net Exports**.
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Short Run Aggregate Supply</mark>: <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Nominal wages</mark> are fixed, and it is assumed that the current price level will remain the same. ==**To shift this curve to the right, the price level can rise and wages remain fixed**==, while conversely ==**to shift it to the left wages can rise and price levels can fall**==.
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Long Run Aggregate Supply</mark>: <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Nominal wages</mark> are flexible, and so are price levels. ==**As price levels rise, so do nominal wages, and vice versa, causing real output to stay the same**==. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">This is the reason why the aggregate supply curve is a vertical line</mark>, while <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">the aggregate demand curve is still downward-sloping</mark>.
* <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Demand-Pull Inflation</mark>: When an increase in aggregate demand pulls price levels upwards.
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Cost-Push Inflation</mark>: Is caused by an increase in input prices leading the aggregate supply curve to shift left.
* <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Recession</mark>: Occurs when there's a decrease in aggregate demand.
* ==**<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">AD</mark>-<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">AS</mark> Model**==: Here the $x$-axis is the ==**GDP**== and the $y$-axis is the price level. As price levels rise, demand falls, so there is an <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">inverse relationship between demand and price level</mark>. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Conversely increased price level increase supply</mark>.
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Production Possibilities</mark>: Portrays a graph (looking kind of like half a semi circle in the 1st segment) portraying in the $x$-axis ==Consumer Goods== and in the $y$-axis, ==Capital Goods==. The larger the circle, the more production possibilities. The way we increase production possibilities is if there is <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">for supply</mark> is :
	* More Resources
	* Better Resources
		* Human Resource: Education, Quantity and Quality
		* More Capital
		* Better ability in business management
		* More natural resources
	* Better Technology
# Is Government Intervention Necessary?
If the prices and wages are downwardly flexible, that means that over time the prices and wages will go down on their own without government intervention.

## The Trade-Off of Unemployment and Inflation
The Phillips Curve represents the short-run trade-off between unemployment and inflation. As unemployment rises, inflation falls, and as inflation rises, unemployment falls.
* ==**Stagflation**==: Is when both inflation and unemployment rises. This is usually caused by <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">supply shocks</mark>.
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Aggregate Supply Shocks</mark>: Sudden increases in the cost of resources. When stagflation occurs, there's not really an obvious solution.
* <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Disinflation</mark>: Not to be confused with deflation, as it is still in inflation. ==**This is characterized by having a decrease in the amount of inflation**==.
