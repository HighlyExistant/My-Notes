A gear is an amazing way to portray rotational kinetic energy in a system. A few parts of a gear are:
1. $a$ or $m$: ==**Addendum or Module of the gear**==: describes a unit of size which will be used as the ratio of all gear sizes to be used alongside other gears you make. **Distance between the reference gear (diameter of the gear), and the tip. Calculated as** $\frac{1}{P}$ or $\frac{p}{\pi}$.
2. $b$: ==**Dedundum**==: Distance between the reference gear (diameter of the gear), and the root. Preferably calculated as $\frac{1.25}{P}$.
3. $z$: ==**Tooth number**==: How many teeth your gears have.
4. $d$: ==**Diameter of the gear**==: calculated as $d=m\cdot z$
5. $d_r$: ==**Root diameter (Base Diameter)**==: The diameter of the circle surrounded by the gears. Calculated as $d-2b$.
6. $da$: ==**Tip diameter (Outside Diameter)**==: The diameter up until the tip of a gear tooth. Calculated as $d+2a$.
7. $p$: ==**Circular Pitch**==: When does the gear teeth repeat. Calculated as $\frac{(D\pi)}{N}$. Could be simplified to $m\pi$. 
8. $P$: ==**Diametral Pitch**==: How long until a new gear tooth will be placed. Calculated as $\frac{\pi}{p}$ or $\frac{N}{d}$.
